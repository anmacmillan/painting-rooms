# Caravaggio's Rome, about 1600: findings

Compiled 15 Nov 2026 for `caravaggio/street.html` ("The Painter's Light"). Each statement names its source. Wikipedia texts were fetched the same day through the MediaWiki API (`prop=extracts`) and are kept in `research/wiki/*.json`. "Owner" means checked by the owner against Wikipedia's article summaries before the build.

## The three paintings

| Statement | Source |
|---|---|
| The Calling of Saint Matthew was completed 1599–1600 for the Contarelli Chapel in San Luigi dei Francesi, where it remains | Owner; en.wikipedia "The Calling of Saint Matthew" (lead) |
| Commission dated July 1599, final payment July 1600 | en.wikipedia "The Calling of Saint Matthew" |
| It shows Matthew at a table with four other men; Christ and Peter have entered; Christ points; a beam of light falls on the men at the table. Del Monte obtained the commission for him | en.wikipedia "The Calling of Saint Matthew" |
| Madonna di Loreto (Pilgrims' Madonna), c.1604–1606, Cavalletti Chapel, Sant'Agostino: the barefoot Virgin holding her naked child in a doorway before two kneeling peasants on pilgrimage | Owner; en.wikipedia "Madonna di Loreto (Caravaggio)" |
| The Cavalletti heirs commissioned it in 1603; it "caused the common people to make a great cackle" | en.wikipedia "Madonna di Loreto (Caravaggio)" |
| Two versions of Medusa, 1596 and c.1597; the Uffizi one is "often dated 1597"; painted on a round poplar shield covered in linen; commissioned by Francesco Maria del Monte as a gift for Ferdinando I de' Medici; now in the Uffizi | Owner; en.wikipedia "Medusa (Caravaggio)" |
| The Medusas are self-portraits (the painter's line "My own face, boy" rests on this) | en.wikipedia "Medusa (Caravaggio)" |
| Ferdinando de' Medici was Grand Duke of Tuscany and a former cardinal | en.wikipedia "Francesco Maria del Monte" |

## The painter and the places

| Statement | Source |
|---|---|
| Caravaggio was living at Palazzo Madama with Cardinal del Monte on 28 November 1600 | en.wikipedia "Caravaggio" |
| Del Monte's Palazzo Madama household; the palace is today the seat of the Italian Senate | en.wikipedia "Francesco Maria del Monte"; "Palazzo Madama, Rome"; Owner |
| A notice of 1604, describing his life three years earlier: he would "swagger about for a month or two with a sword at his side and a servant following him" (the boy is that servant; the boy himself is invented) | en.wikipedia "Caravaggio" |
| He liked dark clothes and went out late at night; arrested 4 May 1598 for carrying a sword ("I carry the sword by right because I am Painter to Cardinal del Monte") | en.wikipedia "Medusa (Caravaggio)" |
| San Luigi dei Francesi: built 1518–1589, façade by Giacomo della Porta, consecrated 8 October 1589 (so it stood in 1600) | en.wikipedia "San Luigi dei Francesi" |
| Sant'Agostino: completed 1483, travertine façade by Giacomo di Pietrasanta (so it stood in 1600) | en.wikipedia "Basilica of Sant'Agostino" |
| Palazzo Madama's present Baroque front, by Paolo Maruscelli, was finished in 1642, replacing an asymmetrical earlier front | it.wikipedia "Palazzo Madama (Roma)" |
| Sant'Agnese in Agone: begun 1652 | en.wikipedia "Sant'Agnese in Agone" |
| Sant'Ivo alla Sapienza: built 1642–1660 | en.wikipedia "Sant'Ivo alla Sapienza" |
| Fountain of the Four Rivers: designed 1651, unveiled 12 June 1651 | en.wikipedia "Fontana dei Quattro Fiumi" |
| Corso del Rinascimento: planned in the 1931 plan, works opened 21 April 1936, replacing the winding Via del Pino, Via del Pinnacolo and Via della Sapienza | it.wikipedia "Corso del Rinascimento" |
| The basin at the north end of Piazza Navona is Giacomo della Porta's of 1574; the Neptune in it was added in 1878 | en.wikipedia "Piazza Navona" |

What the level does with these: Sant'Agnese, Sant'Ivo and the Four Rivers fountain are left out (the OSM footprint "Obelisco Agonale" is skipped; Sant'Agnese's footprint is raised as a plain block like any other, 100 m off the route). The basin of 1574 stands plain at the start. The Corso is walked as a lane and its name is taken off the signs. The end card says all this.

## Not verified (treat as unknown)

- That Via del Salvatore, the Corsia Agonale, Via della Scrofa and Via di Sant'Agostino ran where they run now, under those names, in 1600. The signs carry today's OpenStreetMap names.
- What Palazzo Madama's front looked like in 1600 (only that it was different and asymmetrical). The level shows a plain stone wall and a door.
- That a silver scudo was struck in Rome about 1600. en.wikipedia "Roman scudo" lists a silver 1 scudo among the Papal States' denominations but the text fetched gives no date for it.
- That the Medusa left for Florence in 1598 (commonly said; not in the texts fetched). The end card does not say it.
- The dirty feet of the kneeling pilgrim as the cause of the scandal: the article fetched says only that the Virgin and pilgrims are barefoot and that there was an outcry. Not stated in the level.
- The names "Largo Giuseppe Toniolo", "Via Giuseppe Zanardelli" and "Largo della Costituente" are taken off the signs as plainly modern; that is from general knowledge, not a fetched source.
- Sant'Agostino's front stands at the head of a flight of steps today; the level has one step. San Luigi's and Sant'Agostino's fronts are plain stone walls, not portraits of the façades.

## Invented, and said so on the end card

The boy; every word the painter and the passers-by speak; the night walk; the three pictures standing in doorways or in a room open to the square (the paintings were made years apart, about 1597 to 1606, and one was always in a chapel).

## Map

`research/rome_osm.xml` (raw OpenStreetMap API XML) → `tools/prep_rome.py` → `map.json` (457 buildings, 356 street lines, no water). Buildings are closed ways plus the outer rings of building multipolygons (Palazzo Madama is one). Origin 41.8996 N, 12.4731 E.

Route, 400.5 m (the sum of the laid points): north end of Piazza Navona, Corsia Agonale, Piazza Madama and the palace front, Via del Salvatore, Piazza di San Luigi de' Francesi, Via della Scrofa, Via di Sant'Agostino, Piazza di Sant'Agostino. The points on Corsia Agonale, Via del Salvatore, Via della Scrofa and Via di Sant'Agostino are OpenStreetMap's own nodes. The stretches across Piazza Navona, Piazza Madama, Piazza di San Luigi and Piazza di Sant'Agostino are laid by hand between them, because the squares are mapped as areas and not as lines.

## Materials

- House fronts, paving and stone are the Florence level's pictures (`florence/tex/facade_*.jpg`, `paving.jpg`, `stone.jpg`, made with Google's image model), copied into `caravaggio/tex/`. They are Tuscan fronts; by lantern they pass for Roman plaster. `roof.jpg` and the gown pictures were copied but are not used.
- Figures' clothes are painted by `tools/blender/tex_caravaggio.py` from `drapery.jpg`. The figures share the plain painted face of the Munch girls; men are told apart by beards, hats and cloaks.
- The shield in the doorway is 0.92 m across; the real one is about 60 × 55 cm.
