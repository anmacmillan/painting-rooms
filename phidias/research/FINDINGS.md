# Phidias, the Parthenon frieze and the Panathenaic procession, Athens about 438 BC: findings

Written 15 Nov 2026 for the `phidias/` level. Every claim has a source that was fetched and saved on that day, and a status:

- V verified: a source read says it. "V (primary)" means an ancient text or an excavation record; "V (secondary)" means a modern account only.
- P partly verified: the source says something close, or two sources read disagree on a detail, or the claim goes a step beyond the source.
- U unverified: nothing fetched supports it. Where I add what I recall, it is labelled "from memory" and must not be used as fact.
- D disputed: the sources themselves record a scholarly disagreement.

Nothing here rests on memory unless it says so.

| View | Who | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|

| The first Panathenaia, with Kekrops and Erichthonios | Chr. Kardara | PF, about | V |
| Three periods: mythical (west), archaic (north), classical (south); or north archaic and south classical | E. Harrison; E. Simon; L. Beschi (tens on the south for the ten tribes, fours on the north for the four old tribes) | PF, about | V |
| The procession above the Areopagus only, the ship left below | S. Rotroff | PF, about | V |
| The birth of Ion | William St Clair; rejected by Catharine Titi | W | V (secondary) |

Disputed, plainly: who the five figures of the central scene are and whether the cloth is being handed over or folded away; whether the child is a boy or a girl; who the two girls with stools are and whether they carry stools at all; who the ten standing men are; whether one procession at one moment is shown; why things named in the texts are absent (the ship, parasol-bearers, stool-bearers, girl water-carriers, hoplites, allies). PF: "The ancient sources that refer to the frieze are all later than the Parthenon ... At some points, the sources do not agree with the representations themselves." No ancient writer describes the frieze: Pausanias 1.24.5 mentions only the pediments and the statue.

## C. Colour

| Claim | Finding | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| The frieze was painted, with a blue ground | "We do not know for sure what colour the temple sculptures had. It seems that the background of the frieze was blue and the relief figures were colourful. The figures were often adorned with metal fittings that were fixed on to the marble" (PF, colours game). "This was resolved to a great extent by the use of colour, in particular the blue background" (PF, about). | Egyptian blue found by the British Museum in 2009 | Not confirmed as to the year. The 2023 study is verified at one remove. The 2009 date is U (Verri did publish earlier work with this technique, from memory) |
| Metopes | "The background was certainly red, in contrast with triglyphs in medium or dark blue. The characters were painted, with eyes, hair, lips, jewels and draperies raised" | W en_Metopes | V (secondary), weak |
| Ceiling | coffers with "a painted decoration in the centre" | PF, Parthenon | V (secondary) |

Attested: Egyptian blue, traces of white and of a purple, on the pediment figures and one frieze figure; bronze attachments. Reconstruction: the blue ground of the frieze, the red ground of the metopes, every colour of skin, hair, horse and cloth. Verri: "it is still difficult to say exactly how the artworks would have appeared" (Arch).

## H. Athena Parthenos

Pausanias 1.24.5 to 7 (Jones): "As you enter the temple that they name the Parthenon, all the sculptures you see on what is called the pediment refer to the birth of Athena, those on the rear pediment represent the contest for the land between Athena and Poseidon. The statue itself is made of ivory and gold. On the middle of her helmet is placed a likeness of the Sphinx ... and on either side of the helmet are griffins in relief." (1.24.5) "The statue of Athena is upright, with a tunic reaching to the feet, and on her breast the head of Medusa is worked in ivory. She holds a statue of Victory about four cubits high, and in the other hand a spear; at her feet lies a shield and near the spear is a serpent. This serpent would be Erichthonius. On the pedestal is the birth of Pandora in relief." (1.24.7) Section 6 is a digression on griffins. The Perseus Greek of 1.24.5 prints χρησμοῦ where χρυσοῦ is meant: an error of the digital text.

Pliny, *NH* 36.18 (Bostock and Riley, 36.4): "his Athenian Minerva, six and twenty cubits in height, and composed of ivory and gold; but it is to the shield of this last statue that we shall draw attention; upon the convex face of which he has chased a combat of the Amazons, while, upon the concave side of it, he has represented the battle between the Gods and the Giants. Upon the sandals again, we see the wars of the Lapithæ and Centaurs ... The figure of Victory, in particular, is most admirable, and connoisseurs are greatly struck with the serpent and the sphinx in bronze lying beneath the point of the spear."

## J. What the children were already taught in athens-quest
