# Florence, Dante and Beatrice: findings

Gathered 13 November 2026 by a research agent; every point below was fetched from the source given.

## Vita Nuova (Rossetti's translation)
https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dante_and_His_Circle,_with_the_Italian_Poets_Preceding_Him/The_New_Life
- First sight: Dante near the end of his ninth year, she at the start of hers; she wore "a subdued and goodly crimson".
- Greeting: "nine years exactly" later she appeared "dressed all in pure white, between two gentle ladies elder than she", and "passing through a street" saluted him at the ninth hour.
- Refusal: because of a "false and evil rumour" about his screen-lady, she "denied me her most sweet salutation".
- Death: given by cipher in the text. Wikipedia gives 8 or 19 June 1290 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice_Portinari); the frame of Dante's Dream reads "Junii die 9 anno 1290".
- The 1283 date for the greeting is inferred (born 1265, plus eighteen); the text does not state it.

## Holiday, Dante and Beatrice (Walker Art Gallery, 1882–84)
https://www.liverpoolmuseums.org.uk/artifact/dante-and-beatrice
- Subject: Beatrice refusing to greet Dante (the later meeting, not the greeting of 1283).
- Figures and models: Beatrice in white (Eleanor Butcher), Monna Vanna (Milly Hughes), a maidservant (Kitty Lushington).
- He visited Florence in 1881 and set the scene "at the Ponte Santa Trinita looking towards the Ponte Vecchio along the Lungarno": a brick-paved Lungarno, shops, and a scaffolded Ponte Vecchio, which he believed was destroyed in 1235 and being rebuilt around 1285–90. He made plaster statuettes of the two women.

## Rossetti
- Salutation of Beatrice, 1859: two panels from a Red House cabinet, the meeting in Florence and the meeting in Paradise; National Gallery of Canada, Ottawa (https://www.rossettiarchive.org/docs/s116.rap.html).
- Salutation of Beatrice, 1880–81: Jane Morris as Beatrice; Toledo Museum of Art (https://www.rossettiarchive.org/docs/s260.rap.html).
- Dante's Dream (1869–71): Love leads Dante to the dead Beatrice; his largest painting; Walker Art Gallery (https://www.liverpoolmuseums.org.uk/artifact/dantes-dream).
- Beata Beatrix (c.1864–70, Tate; replica 1871–72, Art Institute of Chicago): Beatrice at her death, modelled on Elizabeth Siddal (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beata_Beatrix).
- The First Anniversary of the Death of Beatrice, 1853: watercolour of Dante interrupted while drawing an angel; Ashmolean (https://www.rossettiarchive.org/docs/s58.rap.html).
- Dantis Amor, 1860: unfinished centre panel of the same cabinet; Tate (https://www.rossettiarchive.org/docs/s117.rap.html).

## Period checks for the 1280s
- Palazzo Vecchio: begun 1299; tower about 1310 (https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Vecchio).
- Loggia dei Lanzi: built 1376–82.
- Piazza della Signoria: cleared about 1268, unpaved until 1385.
- Ponte Vecchio: present bridge about 1339–45; its predecessor, rebuilt after 1177, was swept away in 1333.
- Ponte Santa Trinita: present bridge 1567–69; a wooden bridge of 1252 was replaced in stone after the 1269 flood and fell in 1333.
- Cathedral: begun 1296; dome 1420–36. No dome and no present cathedral in 1283.
- Santa Trinita church: built 1258–80.

## Unverified or doubtful
- Holiday's 1235 flood and 1285–90 rebuilding of the Ponte Vecchio: not corroborated.
- Whether a riverside street existed on the Lungarno line in the 1280s.
- The exact position of the Palazzo Vecchio tower (OSM does not map it separately).
- Dates not checked on the museums' own pages: Ottawa, Ashmolean, Sydney, Chicago.
