## What the level teaches

The one thing: Munch painted what was felt, not what was there. The sky, the shoreline and the colour carry the feeling.

| Statement | Status | Source and quotation |
|---|---|---|
| The Scream's sky and landscape are a feeling, by his own account | verified | NM Scream, quoting his notebook (emunch.no, 1892, MM.T.2367): "I was walking along the road with two friends -- the sun was setting -- suddenly the sky turned blood red -- I paused and leaned on the fence in utter exhaustion -- there was blood and tongues of fire above the blue-black fjord and the city -- my friends walked on, and I stood there trembling with anxiety -- and I sensed an infinite scream passing through nature." |
| Colour not as seen, and form made to say what the subject says | verified | NM Scream: "This approach, along with the vibrant, non-realistic colours, signified a new way of creating art. The Scream marks a decisive point in art history where form and content are closely interrelated and are meant to express the same subject matter." |
| The Scream, 1893, National Museum, Oslo; the road is Ekeberg above the fjord | verified | NM Scream: creation date 1893; "The simplified landscape is recognisable as a representation of the Kristianiafjord as seen from Ekeberg" |
| The Girls on the Bridge is a calmer picture, simplified, of Åsgårdstrand | verified | NM Girls: "The figures, landscape and building in the background are painted in a simplified manner"; "pale pink, light blue and muted dark green of the landscape"; his words on the town: "Summer arrived -- with its intense colours" |
| "It is quiet", "calm" as the feeling of The Girls on the Bridge | the level's reading | NM Girls describes harmony and decoration; it does not name a feeling |

What she does. Dread is one number, and the land is that number: it bends the lines, heats the colours, tilts the view and (new) slows her feet by up to 15 per cent. (1) The Scream is made only when her dread is above 0.8: she has to go up to the figure and let the sky turn. She cannot pass him first; four metres beyond him she is stopped and a sister cries "Look at the sky!". (2) From then she carries the fear all the way down the road: a floor of 0.5 under her dread, felt in full when she is 16 m or more from her sisters and not at all within 4 m, so that the road lies still while she keeps up with them and bends and reddens when she falls behind. (3) The Girls on the Bridge is made only when the fear is put down and the world lies still: at the pier she must stand within 4.5 m of her sisters at the rail for two and a half seconds; the bag then shows dread and calm side by side. If she hangs back, the picture waits, a sister calls "Come and stand with us! It is quiet here." every seven seconds, and the line at the foot of the screen says what to do.

Simplified: one feeling on one dial; fear and calm only. That fear makes the lines writhe is Munch's Scream; that company makes them still is the level's.

The game's own: the carried fear and its numbers; the slowing; the sisters' lines; the idea that Munch's two pictures are the two ends of one evening.
