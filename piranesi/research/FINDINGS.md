Wikipedia is a secondary source: where a claim rests on it alone, that is said. Not obtained: the Metropolitan Museum's essay and the Princeton University Art Museum page (both refused automated requests), and no printed catalogue (Hind, Focillon, Robison, Wilton-Ely) was seen.

Status words: **verified** (read in a saved source), **partly verified** (one secondary sentence, or the source supports only part), **unverified** (not found; not stated as fact in the level), **by looking** (read by eye from the plate files).

## What changed between the states of plate VII (by looking at the two files side by side)

Only in the 1761 file: the timber bridge on V-shaped trestles below the drawbridge; a further gallery crossing the top; many more receding galleries and arches in the distance; a rebuilt, darker foreground; much heavier shadow everywhere. In both: the drawbridge with its rings, the round tower and lantern, the winding stair round a pier, the stone arch below, the spiked wheel, the long crossing ropes.

## What is the game's own (said on the end card)

- The girl, her three drawings, the copper plates in the floor, and the whole device of drawing pieces in. In the game she adds a stone arch, a timber bridge and a winding stair; in the real plate only the timber bridge is an addition of 1761. The arch and the winding stair were there in 1750.
- The ruins in the first scene: a half-buried triumphal arch, a temple's steps and columns, a masons' hoist, a broken aqueduct and pines. They are the kinds of thing he etched in the Vedute di Roma, arranged as no real place. No single Veduta was used as a source (Commons was rate-limiting by then): unverified as topography, and the card says so. The lettering S·P·Q·R on the arch is an impression.
- Piranesi's spoken line apart from the motto ("Bene! Now come and see what I make of them.") and the narrator's sentences without quotation marks.
- The route through the hall, the capstan that lowers the drawbridge, the stone balls on the stair, the swinging hooks, the missing planks, the gold coins lying about, and the lantern that lights at the end.
- The dress of the girl, of Piranesi and of the small figures: an impression, no source.
- The staging differs from the plate in proportion (the pier is larger, the drawbridge shorter) and the upper galleries are simplified.
- Nothing by M. C. Escher was used or imitated.
