Takiyasha the Witch and the Skeleton Spectre

Utagawa Kuniyoshi designed this three-sheet colour woodblock print (nishiki-e) in Edo around 1844. This impression hangs in the British Museum in London (1915,0823,0.915–916); another is in the Victoria and Albert Museum.

What the print shows: after the rebel warlord Taira no Masakado was defeated in AD 939, his daughter Princess Takiyasha stayed in his ruined palace at Sōma. On the left sheet she reads a spell from a scroll to summon a giant skeleton spectre, which rips away the tattered palace blinds to frighten the court warrior Ōya no Tarō Mitsukuni and his companion.

How a colour woodblock print is made: a designer drew the picture on paper and a block cutter carved the black outlines into cherry wood to make the key block. From black-and-white key-block proofs, separate blocks were carved for each colour, with tiny registration notches (kentō) cut into the margin so the printer could line every colour up over the black outline before rubbing the handmade paper with a bamboo pad.

The game’s own: a real Edo colour print typically used ten to sixteen woodblocks; here the print’s own colours are separated into five blocks so you can print the whole triptych in one walk. The girl crossing the floorboards and the layered depth of the ruined palace are the game’s own.

Picture: Utagawa Kuniyoshi (died 1861), public domain, British Museum impression via Wikimedia Commons. Woodblock printing facts verified from the Victoria & Albert Museum and the Library of Congress.
